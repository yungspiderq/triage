# Triage

> **Seven body parts, each with its own health, instead of one health bar.**
> A hit lands *somewhere*. What it does depends on where — and on what you do next.

Minecraft tells you that you have twenty hearts and nothing else. It cannot tell you that
your leg is broken, that the bone in it has moved, that your arm has stopped working, or
that the cut you ignored an hour ago is why you are shivering now.

This mod can.

---

## The body

| Part | Health | What losing it costs you |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Head** | 10 | dizziness, then darkness — *empty it and you go down* |
| **Thorax** | 22 | everything heals slower — *empty it and you go down* |
| **Stomach** | 16 | food burns away far faster |
| **Arms** | 12 each | slower mining, weaker and slower attacks |
| **Legs** | 14 each | slower, and you can barely jump |

The five parts that are not vital **black out** instead of killing you. They sit at zero,
refuse ordinary healing, and quietly drag your chest down every second until you deal
with them.

Armour, resistance and enchantments all keep working exactly as they did — the mod only
changes where the damage goes *afterwards*. A blow that lands where you are wearing
nothing hurts about a third more, and **nothing covers your arms**, which is why arms are
the limbs that go first.

Press **H** — **J** if Sable: Ragdolls is installed, which wants H for itself — or click
the red cross beside your inventory. Hovering a limb says what is wrong with it, one line
and one icon per thing.

---

## Bleeding has three grades, and the third is not a faster second

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **A light cut** | closes on its own, given a minute nobody spends bleeding |
| **A heavy bleed** | never clots. A bandage, a hand, or a strap |
| **An artery** | **4.5% of your blood a second** |

An artery is a different injury rather than a worse one. It goes with the heart, it is a
brighter red because that blood has not been anywhere yet, and **it does not clot, ever** —
a dressing over one soaks through and keeps soaking. A bleed in a thigh blacks the leg out
in six seconds and puts you on the floor inside thirty.

You can tell one across a clearing without opening anything: it **pulses** on the body, it
throws particles sideways in time with the heart instead of dripping down, and it has its
own colour and its own icon on the chart.

There are exactly two answers and you already carry both — **a hand pushed hard into it**,
or **a strap above it**. While the hand is there, nothing comes out at all.

## Bones: broken, moved, or outside

Three rungs, three different jobs.

**Broken, in line.** Two pieces where they were. A splint is the whole answer.

**Out of line.** The ends have moved, so the limb is the wrong shape — and that is visible
on the body from across a room. A splint here holds the bone precisely where it should not
be, and a week later that is permanent. Something has to pull it straight first.

**Bone through the skin.** Also a wound: it bleeds, it will not close while there is bone
in the way, and it is three times the road to infection. No splint goes on it and nothing
is set. A surgeon cleans it out and closes it, and what is left is an ordinary displaced
fracture.

### Setting one

Bare hands, three moves: **pull** along the bone until the ends can pass each other,
**line it up** while the pull is holding, then **let it down slowly** — the ends seat as
the pull comes off, and a hand taken away all at once drops them together however they
were lying.

It is the most painful thing in the mod and **the only treatment the room can hear**. The
noise brings whatever is out there. A patient under the mask makes none.

### And if you leave it

A bone left out of line knits out of line. That part is **worth less for good** — the only
permanent loss of health in the mod — it walks slower, and it aches on the weather louder
than a straight bone ever did. One way out: a surgeon breaks it again, on purpose, so that
this time somebody can set it straight.

## What else can be wrong with you

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **Blood loss** | what bleeding leaves behind — rest and food, a drip, or somebody else's blood |
| **Concussion** | while it lasts you cannot read your own condition |
| **Infection** | what an untreated wound becomes, and it has somewhere to go |
| **Burns** | three depths, and the depth decides everything |
| **A crushed lung** | nothing to see, gets worse the longer it waits, one answer only |
| **Torn organs** | eight of them, each at its own rate, and no bandage reaches any |
| **Illness** | colds, fever, pneumonia, food poisoning, worms, rabies |
| **Shock, hypothermia, sleeplessness** | a body failing rather than a body wounded |
| **Lockjaw** | out of a puncture closed over dirt; silent while it is treatable |
| **Crush injury** | what comes back out of a limb when the strap finally comes off |
| **Relapsing fever** | a course that was not finished, and a fever that now lies to a thermometer |

### The chain

```
open wound  →  infection  →  fever  →  blood poisoning
```

Blood poisoning does not pass on its own, and it takes your chest apart while you decide
what to do about it. **You can put treatment off. You cannot cancel it.**

### Three that are not about dirt

**Lockjaw** is the only thing here that cares what *shape* a wound is: a deep narrow hole
from an arrow or a fragment, dressed over dirt. It is silent, and it is silent for exactly
as long as it is treatable. The one tell is not a reading and not an instrument — it is a
sentence. Ask them, and a patient whose jaw is stiffening answers slowly. After that the
course stops working, and the spasms start, and a spasm is the only thing in this mod that
interrupts a treatment from the inside.

**Crush injury** is caused by doing the right thing too late. A limb that has been under a
strap fills up with what a living limb flushes away, and releasing it sends all of that to
the heart at once. The answer is the bag of saline you should have hung *before* you took
the strap off.

**Relapsing fever** is the one that came back, because one bottle is one dose and a dose is
not a course. It comes in waves — and a fever that comes in waves is **normal at the bottom
of its cycle**, so a thermometer used at the wrong moment is honest, accurate and wrong.
Measure twice.

### A burn has a depth

Red skin closes by itself. Blistered is the loudest thing on a burnt body and wants salve.
Through the skin **does not hurt at all** — the nerves that would have done the hurting are
part of what burnt — and it wants a knife. So the patch somebody is screaming about is the
survivable one, and the quiet one beside it is not. You find that one by pressing on them
and watching for the place that does not answer.

A burn is not over when the fire is out: it goes on deciding how deep it is for half a
minute more, and water is the only thing that changes that.

---

## A chest you can hear, and a patient who will not hold still

Kneel next to somebody and you can **hear** whether their chest is working - not the rate
and not the cause, just that something is wrong with it. A wet chest, a chest that cannot
finish a breath and a chest working far too hard all sound different. A chest that has
stopped makes no sound at all, and one day you will notice that.

And an awake patient in real pain does not lie still for an operation. They pull away, and
the screen closes. There are three answers and the mod already had all of them: ether,
painkillers, or a friend crouching beside you with an empty hand to hold them. Any one of
them, and it never happens.

## Medicine you have to actually perform

Twenty-odd items, and **none of them work by simply clicking**. Using one opens a screen
where you pick the part yourself — and then do the work. The screen does not pause the
game. All craftable, and all for sale from a village medic.

| Item | The work |
|:---|:---|
| **Bandage** | wind it round the limb, three times round |
| **Tourniquet** | two hard turns of strap — bleeding stops dead, the limb starts dying |
| **Splint** | a board long enough, and two ties, above and below — never over the break |
| **Medkit** | four points along the wound, each before its ring closes |
| **Antiseptic** | scrub it out, edge to edge |
| **Burn salve** | cover the burn without wasting any — there is barely enough |
| **Surgical kit** | clean the field, draw the fragment or the round up the channel, stitch it shut |
| **Bone saw** | the one treatment nobody wants to do |
| **Forceps** | fragments and bullets, one at a time, without touching the sides |
| **Scissors** | cut a stuck dressing away instead of tearing it off |
| **Thermometer** | shake it down, hold it still, read it |
| **Saline drip** | find the vein and stay in it while the bag empties |
| **Blood bag** | the same needle, in whichever direction the blood is going |
| **Anaesthetic** | the mask: hold the bag on their breathing, not on a clock |
| **Decompression needle** | between the ribs, once, in the right place |
| **Adrenaline, morphine, naloxone** | three needles that do not forgive |
| **Antibiotics, worming, antipsychotics** | courses, which have to be finished |
| **Painkillers** | swallowed. Hides the pain, heals nothing |
| **Prosthetics** | a peg, a hook, a jointed leg, a mechanical hand — and they wear out |
| **Medical bracelet** | stamped with what you cannot have, read off you while you cannot talk |

Go back the way you came and the bandage comes off again. Three slips end the attempt. So
does being set upon while you work.

**Nothing is ever lost but time.**

## Empty hands are not nothing

Nine things anybody can do with nothing in their pockets: **hold pressure** on a wound,
**open an airway**, **push a heart round**, **roll somebody into the recovery position**,
**pull an arrow out**, **take a dressing or a strap off**, **set a displaced bone**,
**work a stiff joint loose**, and **take a pulse**.

None of them repairs anything. What they change is who can help: the answer used to be
"whoever packed a surgical kit this morning", and now it is "whoever is here".

---

## One reading is a number. Two are an answer.

Every reading anybody takes off a body goes on an **observation sheet**, with the time on it
and the name of whoever took it. Open it from the chart.

A pulse of 112 means nothing on its own. A pulse of 112 on somebody who was at 90 four
minutes ago is bleeding somewhere, and there is no wound on the outside to find — a torn
organ bleeds inwards, silently, and until now the only way to find one was to open somebody
up.

The chart prints an arrow once there is something to compare against, and the mod says the
difference out loud as you take it — never what it means. A climbing pulse is also a
frightened person, and a person who has been running, and a fever on its way up. Which one
it is, is your job.

It is the only finding in the mod whose price is **patience** rather than an item: a
direction costs a second visit and there is no other way to get one.

And the name in the third column is how handover works. The second medic to arrive can see
what the first one already did.

---

## A room that watches

A ward is not a place with better medicine in it. It is a place where somebody writes a
number on a page every so often.

A patient lying beside a medical table has their pulse and temperature taken automatically,
quietly, on a clock. Ten minutes in a clinic and the sheet is a column of numbers going the
wrong way — which is the first reason in this mod to carry somebody somewhere. Supplies
travel. A place that measures for you does not.

---

## Readings, and why they disagree

- **A pulse** is a fact about a body — two fingers, no item — and a fast thin one on
  somebody with nothing open is how you find the bleeding you cannot see.
- **Breathing** is counted the same way.
- **A temperature** is a figure from an instrument, and it costs the instrument.
- **Where does it hurt** comes out of the patient's mouth, so it stops working the moment
  they stop being able to talk — and painkillers make them under-report.
- **Out of ten** is an opinion, and it is wrong in more ways than anything else here.

A two out of ten on a heart running at a hundred and thirty is not a comfortable patient.
It is one somebody has already given something to.

## Somebody in your group is O−

Everybody has a blood type, **rolled again every time they respawn**. Blood may only go
into somebody who already carries every antigen it does — the real ABO and Rh rules, which
nobody had to invent to make interesting.

Draw blood into an empty bag and the donor pays for it out of their own volume. Give a
matching bag and it puts back far more than saline can. Give the **wrong** bag and the body
attacks it, and no course of anything shortens that.

> A stockpile of matched blood stops matching the moment its owner dies.

## And somebody is allergic to bread

Ten allergies are rolled at spawn and **seven of them are food** — fish, wheat, egg, milk,
honey, mushrooms, berries — with the other three in your own bag: antibiotics, morphine,
anaesthetic.

A drug allergy is somebody else's problem to remember about you. A food allergy is yours,
every time you open your own inventory, and nothing warns you: you eat it, you gain the
hunger, and then your throat starts closing. The answer to both is the same needle.

## What a body keeps

Every injury used to heal back into the same body it started as. Somebody who had survived
four amputations, a septic belly and a skull fracture was, once mended, indistinguishable
from somebody who had never been touched.

Parts remember now:

- **A knitted bone** is not weaker — but it is where the next fall lands, and it **knows
  the weather**. Old breaks ache before rain, and ache indoors, because what they answer to
  is the pressure rather than the wet.
- **A scar** is not skin. There is less blood going through it, which is exactly why the
  next infection settles where the last one did.
- **A deep burn tightens** as it closes, and a limb sewn back on never straightens the way
  it did.

The history is permanent and only dying clears it. The **stiffness is not**: somebody takes
the joint out to the edge of what it will do and holds it there, one session a day, over
days. Short of the edge nothing loosens; past it something tears. The edge is not drawn
anywhere, because the only thing that knows where it is, is the person whose arm it is —
so the whole instrument is their face.

## What it looks like from inside

Volume going closes the edges of the screen in and takes the colour out of what is left,
and the whole picture dims and comes back **on your own pulse** — a screen that has started
beating quickly is a reading nobody had to take. A head that has been hit will not hold the
horizon still. Fever washes the room warm; cold goes pale and shivers.

And if the body decides it cannot keep your head supplied while standing up, it stops
standing up: **you faint**, the screen goes black, and seven seconds later you are fine.
Nothing is damaged and nothing needs treating. What it costs is the seven seconds and
wherever you happened to be — on a ledge, in water, or in front of something that was
already swinging at you.

Sweat is not drawn — nobody has ever seen their own face. What a hot body gives you is what
sweat does to the person producing it: it runs down, it gets into an eye, and it stings, and
for a second you are squinting at the knot you were tying.

And the last one is not on the screen at all. Ask anybody who has fainted what happened
first and they do not say the edges closed in — they say **the voices went far away**. So as
the volume goes, so does the room: the world muffles, and a player who has stopped reading
the picture still notices that.

All of it has a strength slider and an off switch, and the camera movement can be turned
off separately from the picture.

## Going down, and being got up

Empty a head or a chest and you do not die — you go on the floor with a clock. Mobs lose
interest. Anybody can hold your airway open, push your heart round, roll you onto your
side, pack the wound that is emptying you, or pick you up and carry you somewhere quieter.

Carrying is slow, it gets slower the longer you carry, and you cannot fight while you do
it. If nobody comes, the clock runs out.

Install **Sable: Ragdolls** or **Ragdollified** and the pose becomes physics: the body is
dropped at whatever speed it was carrying, lies where it lands, and cannot climb off the
doll until the downing ends. Neither is required.

## Somebody's sword, in somebody's leg

Rarely, a blade heavy enough to have gone in deep stays in - and whoever swung it does not
have it any more. It is the only disarm in the mod and it happens to the winner of the
fight.

While it is in, it is **holding the wound shut**: the wound it made is not bleeding because
the thing that made it is still in the hole. Nothing bandages over it, nothing clots around
it. So the injury is getting it out, and that is a real decision made at the worst possible
moment - bare hands now, and the worst bleed in the mod, or a kit in a minute and one a
dressing answers.

It is also the one piece of first aid everybody has heard and nobody believes: *do not pull
it out.* And you can see it from across a field, on somebody who is still walking around.

## What you can see from the doorway

Everything else in this mod is fetched: walk over, kneel down, put something on somebody.
Two things are not, and they are the two a real medic uses before any of it — **the colour
of the skin and the colour of the lips**.

They are drawn on the body, and they read across a room. **White is volume. Blue is air.**
They are never the same emergency and they want opposite things done first, so a doorway
full of casualties sorts itself before you have touched anybody.

---

## Sorting the wounded, which is what the word means

**Right-click a player with an empty hand** and you get their chart. Nothing is spent and
nothing is healed: you looked.

Then write on them. A **triage tag** pins a colour to their chest that everybody can read
from a distance — *walking*, *needs help*, *now*, *gone* — with your name on it and how
long ago you wrote it. Stale marks fade. It is the only thing about somebody else's body
that travels without a walk over and a look.

Limbs that are missing, dressings, straps, splints, burns, deformities and blood travel the
same way: they are the shape of somebody, and the shape of somebody is not private.

---

## The guide, and a manikin

Seventy-one pages in thirteen sections, one heading open at a time: bleeding, bones, open
wounds, burns and cold, chest and belly, illness, losing a limb, on the floor, readings,
the bag, the head, you and the room, and what it leaves.

Thirty-eight of those pages have a line at the bottom that says **"Try it, over nobody."**
It opens the real procedure with nothing on the other end of it — no patient, no item
spent, no outcome — because every procedure in this mod is otherwise learnt over somebody
who is dying. Nothing is unlocked by it and nothing is refused without it.

## Two difficulties

**Arcade** is the mod as it has always been, and it is the default: open the body chart and
it tells you the left leg has two breaks and an infection.

**Realistic** changes one thing only — where that knowledge comes from. The body is
identical. What goes is the free answer: a part nobody has examined reads as a **blank**,
and the difference between "nothing there" and "did not look" is the whole of the mode.

Everything needed to fill the chart in was already in the mod, built one version at a time:
a pulse, asking where it hurts, pressing on a burn, a thermometer, an X-ray. Two more arrive
with it — **feeling along a bone**, which is the only way to find a break that has not moved
out of line without a machine, and a **stethoscope**, which is the only way into a chest at
all.

What anybody in the room can see stays visible in both. This hides what is inside somebody,
never what is plain.

## Villages have a medic

Villages generate a **clinic**, built out of whatever that village is built out of: two beds
with an aisle between them, water and the table and a brewing stand and a barrel of supplies
along the far wall, a stone floor under the working half of the room, and a flat roof with a
parapet if the village is a desert one. The villager who takes the job at that table sells
bandages and splints cheaply, antibiotics and salve at expert, and — last of all — saline,
blood bags and surgical kits. Put a medical table down yourself and any unemployed villager
will take the job.

---

## Take as much of it as you want

Everything above is a switch in the server config, so it travels with the world: part
health, where hits land, bleeding rates, blood loss, bones and how far they move,
infection, burns, illness, and every penalty. On NeoForge the settings button on the mod
opens the same file in game, with every setting and its range; Forge and Fabric still use
the file.

Whole subsystems switch off on their own — the minigames, the downed state, carrying, tags,
ragdolls, airways, cardiac arrest, aimed hits, infection, illness, burns, limbs, the
permanent marks, displacement — so a pack can take the parts it wants and leave the rest.
Point the treatments at another mod's items with an item id or a tag and they work.

Natural regeneration has its own switch. Turn it off and food, potions, beacons and every
other vanilla heal stop touching the body; bandages, kits, splints and surgery still work,
because they mend the body directly. Clots are rarer and need a reason, blood trails last
five minutes instead of ninety seconds, and schizophrenia is on by default with a switch
like everything else.

The client settings are separate and belong to whoever is looking: which paper the chart is
printed on, how strongly the symptoms are drawn, and whether the camera moves at all.
